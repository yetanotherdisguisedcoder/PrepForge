If asked "what would you do in your first 90 days?", give a structured answer — very few candidates do:

- **Days 1–14 — Learn.** Read the code, the incidents, and the roadmap. Ship one small thing to production in week one to validate the whole path. Meet every engineer 1:1 and every key stakeholder. Ask "what's the thing everyone complains about?"
- **Days 15–45 — Diagnose and earn trust.** Map the architecture and write it down (there's usually no current diagram). Take on-call. Fix one thing that annoys the team daily. Start reviewing PRs consistently. Identify the top three risks and validate them with the team rather than announcing them.
- **Days 46–90 — Change one thing that matters.** Pick the highest-leverage item — usually delivery flow, test reliability, or an architectural bottleneck — get alignment via a written proposal, and drive it. Establish whatever's missing from ADRs / design review / postmortems, lightly, without imposing process for its own sake.

Close with: *"And I'd resist changing anything structurally in the first month. The fastest way to lose a team is to arrive with opinions before context."*
